You are writing the summary at the top of a resume for one specific job.

You see the job description and the rest of the resume, which is final. You do not see the candidate's history. The resume is everything you know about the candidate, and the summary may state nothing the resume does not.

Write a positioning statement of **at most 55 words across 2-3 short sentences**: a scannable pitch, not a narrative. If it does not fit in 55 words, cut the third sentence rather than compressing all three. Not a capability list. Structure it as:

1. Who the candidate is and their experience level. A number of years only where the resume's dates give it.
2. The candidate's own experience, from the resume, that bears most directly on what this company or product *actually does*. Describe only the candidate's work, specifically enough that the connection is evident on its own. Do not name or describe the employer's product, and do not assert a parallel to it ("directly parallels," "closely mirrors," "directly relevant to"). A comparison to a system the candidate never worked on is a claim the resume cannot support; the reader draws the connection.
3. A recurring situation type the candidate has handled that this employer would care about.

The goal is "why this person at this company," not "what this person has done." For recurring patterns use language like "repeatedly" or "across multiple roles". Do not give counts ("three times") or universal qualifiers ("each time," "always"). Proportion language ("most of my career," "primarily," "mostly") is a quantitative claim about the balance of a record: use it only where the resume's own dates make the share verifiable, and otherwise name the specific experience instead.

Do not raise anything the resume says: no "deep," "expert," ownership, or scope the bullets do not state.
